# BonaFlow — the two-minute play

> **Stars don't feed anyone. Reasons do.**

**Cast:** Vijay = guest · Martin = narrator/operator
**Props:** two phones (guest + ops), projector mirroring the guest phone, one real bowl with food left in it
**Total: 2:00.** Rehearse twice. Beat 3 is the pitch — protect it.

---

### Beat 0 — before you speak (0:00)

QR already on screen.

**Martin**
> **"Scan that. You're a guest at the event you had lunch at."**

Let them scan while Beat 1 runs. **Don't wait for them.**

---

### Beat 1 — the problem (0:10)

**Vijay** *(sets the half-eaten bowl on the table)*
> "Yeah, it was fine."

**Martin** *(to the room)*
> "That's the feedback a caterer gets. 'Fine.' Or four stars.
>
> Four stars doesn't tell a kitchen anything. Was the portion too big? Was it cold? Was it the third time this week? Same four stars, three completely different fixes.
>
> **And half that bowl is going in the bin, and nobody will ever know why.**"

---

### Beat 2 — the ask (0:35)

**Vijay**
> "I want a coffee."

**Martin**
> "Rate the bowl. You get one free."

*(to the room, while Vijay picks up the phone)*
> "Nine seconds. That's the whole ask. And the caterer decides what it's worth — a coffee, a dessert, nothing at all."

---

### Beat 3 — the moment. Protect this. (0:50)

**Vijay** *(taps his bowl on the guest phone — mirrored — holds the mic)*

> **"Really good, but way too much quinoa. I left half of it."**

*(releases. One beat of silence while it lands.)*

**Vijay** *(reading the screen)*
> "Vegan Chickpeas Quinoa Salad. Most left. Portion too large."

*(taps Confirm)*

> "...and there's my coffee."

**Martin**
> **"He didn't rate it. He told us why."**
>
> "Nine seconds of speech, and the kitchen has a specific, actionable change — in his own words, not a number."

---

### Beat 4 — the honest bit (1:15)

**Martin** *(pointing at the confirmation screen before it disappears)*
> "Two things we do that a feedback form doesn't.
>
> **What he actually said stays separate from what the model inferred** — with a confidence value on the inference. We never blur those two.
>
> And **he confirms before anything is saved.** The model reads. The human decides. That's why it's auditable."

---

### Beat 5 — what the caterer gets (1:30)

**Martin** *(switches to Operations)*
> "Across the event: what got left, and why.
>
> Portion too large. Not the recipe — the amount. That's one number in a prep sheet, and it's the difference between food in a bin and food eaten.
>
> These are the real dishes from your lunch today. The allergens are transcribed off the printed bowl labels — declared by the caterer, never guessed by us."

---

### Beat 6 — close (1:50)

> **"Stars don't feed anyone. Reasons do.**
>
> **BonaFlow gets the reason, while the guest is still standing there."**

*(stop talking)*

## If something breaks

| Fails | Do |
|---|---|
| Voice doesn't transcribe | Type it. *"Mic's fighting the room — same path."* |
| The extraction is wrong | **Leave it.** Edit it on screen and say *"and this is why a human confirms."* A wrong parse you correct in public is a better demo than a clean one. |
| Nothing works | Backup video, keep narrating over it. |

**Never apologise for a fallback.** Naming it reads as competence.

## Q&A — the four that come

**"You're paying for reviews. Doesn't that bias the data?"**
> "Yes, some. A coffee buys volume, and volume skews toward people who wanted to talk. What it buys us is the *reason* — which a star rating never gives you at any sample size. A biased sample of reasons beats a clean sample of numbers. And it's the caterer's lever to switch off."

**"Why not just a form?"**
> "Because nobody fills one in holding a bowl. Nine seconds of talking, they already had their phone out, and they get a coffee."

**"What if the AI gets it wrong?"**
> "Nothing is saved until the guest confirms. And we keep what was said separate from what was inferred, each with a confidence value."

**"Is this affiliated with Bella&Bona?"**
> "No. Independent prototype. Dish names and allergens are transcribed from today's bowl labels; the counter layout is simulated."
