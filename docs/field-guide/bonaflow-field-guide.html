01Orientation

Read this page before you write a line of code. It is the whole strategy in one screen.

The core transformation — this must be visible on stage

One sentence becomes five operational changes

“Vegan Thai Curry is almost finished, and around 20 guests are waiting.”

→ Station B availability drops · a high-priority replenishment task is created · the ops dashboard updates · guests are redirected to Station C · a multilingual announcement is generated.

The insight to lead with

The gap is not sensing. The gap is distribution. The person behind Station B already knows the vegan curry is nearly gone. That fact is worth a great deal to 250 guests and to the kitchen — and today it travels no further than the two metres in front of them.

Three seconds of a staff member’s voice becomes a live redirect for every guest in the room, in their own language.

The problem, stated so a caterer nods

Guests Long queues at one station while another stands quiet. Can’t locate vegan or vegetarian choices. International guests miss announcements entirely.
Catering teams Food runs out without operations knowing. Staff coordinate through fragmented conversations. Replenishment is late. Surplus at one station, shortage at another.

Static signs and printed menus cannot respond to live conditions. BonaFlow creates one shared real-time view for guests and catering teams.

Three modes, one app, no authentication

A plain demo-mode selector: Guest · Staff · Operations. No login screen, no password, no role picker.

Why a mode selector and not three apps

It is the reliability path — if the second device dies on stage you switch modes on one phone and the demo still runs. But demo on two devices when you can: the guest view changing untouched while you hold the staff phone is the single strongest moment in the pitch.

Four ways this dies on stage
  • The live update doesn’t fire. Poll at 3 seconds, not realtime magic — and build the manual override at 15:00 (§05).
  • Waiting on the model in front of the room. Quick actions change state with no AI in the path. Pre-generate the announcements.
  • Two devices, two people, one narration. Rehearse the handoff twice.
  • Claiming to detect queues. You don’t, and you don’t need to. Say so — it is the stronger claim.

02The three views

Guest finds food. Staff reports reality. Operations sees everything.

Guest — tabs: Stations · For You · Updates

View all stations, queue status and availability · filter by dietary preference · get a recommended station · read and hear live announcements · a simple location label (“by the stairs”, not a map route) · mark that you are joining a queue.

Dietary filters — support only: All · Vegan · Vegetarian · Gluten-free option · Halal option.

⚠ The line you may never cross
  • Never claim a dish is allergy-safe. Never render “safe” or “you can eat this.”
  • Filter on declared dietaryTags only. The model never decides what a dish contains from its name, its photo, or its own knowledge. It filters a list.
  • No match → say so flatly: “No station currently has a vegan option available.” Never soften, never offer a “probably fine” alternative.
  • Always display: “Dietary labels are demonstration data. Guests must confirm allergens and ingredients with catering staff.”

Staff — tabs: Stations · Report · Tasks

Pick a station, then update by voice, text, optional tray photo, or quick action.

Quick actions
one tap, no AI call		 Stock running low · Item sold out · Replenishment arrived · Queue increasing · Queue cleared · Station temporarily closed

Build quick actions early. They exercise the entire downstream state machine with no OpenAI in the path — they are your latency insurance and your fallback and your 14:15 gate.

Confirm before apply — mandatory

The staff member reviews the extracted update in plain language and confirms before shared data changes. Edit affordance on every field, plus Cancel.

This is not friction, it is the product being honest — and it helps the demo, because the judges watch the extraction land before it takes effect.

When the photo and the human disagree, the human wins — and you show it. A tray can look full because it was just re-stacked with something else; the person standing there knows. The UI says “photo suggested available; staff reported low — using staff.” That one line is worth more than any accuracy claim you could make.

Operations — tabs: Overview · Alerts · Activity

All stations · status · queue level · available dishes · low-stock items · active alerts · replenishment tasks · most crowded station · recommended redirection · recent staff updates · timestamp of the last update.

🟢 GreenAvailable
🟠 OrangeRunning low or busy
🔴 RedSold out, closed or urgent
⚪ GreyNo recent update — build this one. A station nobody has reported on is not “fine”, it is unknown, and showing that is more credible than a board that is green everywhere by default.

03Architecture & the AI contract

Model reads. Code decides. Closed sets everywhere.

Surface — two viable paths, gated by the clock not the hardware

You have two iPhones and one Android, so you can build and demo cross-platform properly, and Bilt’s iOS-only streamed simulator is an asset rather than a liability. Test on both platforms from the first screen — cross-platform bugs cost minutes now and an hour at 15:30.

Bilt / React Native The partner prize, real native camera and mic. Phased prompts in BILT-PROMPT.md. Generate a real RN app, not a web wrapper.
Next.js PWA No build step, no install friction for the room scanning your QR. Also cross-platform — Android Chrome and iOS Safari both give camera and mic.
The Bilt gate, and the one iOS trap that ends demos

If Phase 1 is not rendering on a real phone by 12:45, drop Bilt, say nothing about it on stage, and go all-in on the PWA.

⚠ iPhone ring/silent switch. With it on silent, audio playback is completely silent with no error unless the app sets playsInSilentModeIOS. Set it in code and check the physical switch before you present. On the web path, iOS Safari records mp4/aac, not webm — never hardcode the mime type.

Routes and state

/ mode selector /ops operations dashboard /e/[eventId] guest navigator /api/* server-side only /staff staff update GET /api/event/:id -> { event, stations[], dishes[], alerts[], tasks[] } POST /api/staff-update -> { stationId, transcript?, imageBase64?, quickAction? } POST /api/apply -> { extraction } -> { ok, changed[] } GET /api/guest?diet=vegan -> filtered + ranked

Entities: Event · Station · Dish · StaffUpdate · Alert · ReplenishmentTask.

Supabase, or seeded local state the moment it costs more than 25 minutes. Realtime if free; otherwise poll every 3 seconds and stop thinking about it — polling reads as real-time to an audience and never fails mysteriously. Never in-memory: serverless drops it between requests, the phone writes, the laptop reads nothing, and you lose twenty minutes finding out why.

The sequencing rule that unblocks everything

Agree the data model together, on paper, ten minutes, before either of you types. Then ship a stub POST /api/staff-update returning hardcoded valid JSON within twenty minutes. The UI wires against the stub immediately and never waits.

The AI contract

One server-side call per staff update. Structured Outputs, strict:true. Keys stay server-side. Inputs: transcript · optional tray photo · the closed list of this event’s station and dish ids.

{ "stationId": "station-b", "stationName": "Green Kitchen", "dishId": "vegan-thai-curry", "dishName": "Vegan Thai Curry", "availability": "available | low | sold_out | uncertain", "queueLevel": "low | medium | high | unknown", "reportedGuestCount": 20, "issueType": "low_stock | sold_out | queue | closure | resolved | other", "priority": "low | medium | high | urgent", "reportedFacts": ["string"], "aiInferences": ["string"], "recommendedAction": "string", "recommendedAlternativeStationId": "station-c", "guestAnnouncement": "string", "confidence": 0.0 }
  • Closed sets. stationId and dishId must be ids from the supplied lists — never invented. This single constraint removes most of the hallucination class you would otherwise spend the afternoon debugging.
  • Reported facts and AI inferences never merge. Two visually distinct rows. Every inference carries its confidence.
  • Never present uncertain visual analysis as fact. Photo-only and unsure → it is an inference, not a fact.
  • Never invent a count. reportedGuestCount is only ever a number a human said; otherwise null. No portions, no grams, no wait-time prediction.
  • Validate server-side. On invalid output: keep the transcript, apply nothing, show the correction UI.
The model proposes. Code decides. Say this on stage.

The model returns recommendedAlternativeStationId. Validate it before you ever show it — does that station actually have a matching dish marked available? If the suggestion fails the check, the deterministic rule wins and only code can return “nowhere has this.”

“The model reads. The rules decide. That’s why it’s auditable.” A caterer who has to explain a decision to a client hears that clearly.

Incentives — the lever that turns advice into movement

A redirect on its own is advice, and advice moves maybe a third of a queue. An incentive moves most of it: “Station C has vegan options and a shorter queue — and a free coffee if you head over now.” This is what turns BonaFlow from a dashboard that observes into an operations tool that acts — and it is the answer to “so what?” on the ops side: the manager isn’t just watching Station B go red, they have a lever to rebalance the room.

Offer only what a caterer can absorb: free coffee · free dessert · a drink upgrade · skip-the-queue at a quieter station. Low marginal cost, high perceived value, no kitchen work.

⚠ An incentive is an operational lever, not a rewards programme
  • The model NEVER offers one. It is not in the extraction schema and never comes out of OpenAI. An LLM inventing free dessert promises something the caterer did not authorise. Ops sets it. Full stop.
  • One-off, attached to a specific redirect. No points, balances, streaks, tiers, wallet or redemption history — those need accounts, and a currency invites “what redemption rate do you expect?”, which you cannot answer.
  • Redemption is: show this screen at Station C. No payment, no scan validation, no one-time-use enforcement. If asked about abuse: “it’s a coffee, and the alternative is a 20-minute queue — the caterer decides the risk.”
  • authorizedBy and an expiry are mandatory and both render. An offer with no visible authority and no end time is a promise nobody owns.

It rides along with the redirect that is already firing — a chip under the recommended station and one clause on the announcement. No extra screen, no extra demo step. If you’re behind, cut the ops toggle and hardcode one incentive in the seed data; the pitch line survives intact.

resolveRedirect(dish, fromStation, modelSuggestion): candidates = stations with a matching-dietaryTags dish, availability "available" drop fromStation rank by queueLevel (low < medium < high < unknown), then proximity if modelSuggestion in candidates -> use it else if candidates -> use candidates[0], log the override else -> null, "No station currently has this."

Recovery — build this at 15:00, not at 16:20

The manual override: a hidden control that forces the scripted state change with no network at all. If the parse hangs, tap it and say “the parse is real, I’m firing the cached one.” Honest, and the demo continues.

Devices: iPhone #1 = guest (mirrored, the screen that changes untouched) · iPhone #2 = staff (in hand, off-screen) · Android = operations board and cross-platform proof.

Mirroring, set up at 15:00. iPhone → Mac: QuickTime → File → New Movie Recording → arrow beside record → select the iPhone (needs a cable and tapping Trust; no wifi involved). Android → Mac: scrcpy over USB with USB debugging ON. Both need a data-capable cable — charge-only cables are common and fail silently. Phone hotspot as the primary network.

The floor — the smallest demo that still wins

One laptop, two browser windows. Staff window: quick-action “Item sold out.” Guest window: watch it turn red, re-rank, and hear the announcement.

“Two windows, one laptop — the loop is real, the transport is simple.” A working loop beats a beautiful app with a broken one. Know this floor so you don’t panic at 15:00.

Do not build

Payments · ordering · accounts · GPS or indoor positioning · camera crowd counting · facial recognition · tray-volume measurement · wait-time prediction · real B&B integration · inventory management · delivery logistics · push infrastructure · multi-event · App Store deployment · ratings or gamification. Not a complaint box — guests report availability, not grievances. Not a nutrition tracker. No allergen or calorie estimation from a photo, ever. Not a rewards programme — the incentive is a one-off lever on a redirect; no points, balances, streaks, tiers, wallet or redemption history.

Pitch — three minutes

Hook: “At large events, the problem is rarely that there is no food. The problem is that guests and catering teams don’t know what’s happening across every station.” → Problem (queues, shortages, dietary confusion, fragmented comms) → Product → Live demo → Business value (guest experience · faster response · better distribution · fewer shortages · directional waste reduction · structured event data — no euro figures, you cannot substantiate one) → Technology, one sentence each, then “the model reads, the rules decide — that’s why it’s auditable” → Expansion (conferences, campuses, festivals, stadiums, universities, trade fairs) → Close: “BonaFlow turns every catering station into a connected, responsive part of the event.”

Q&A pocket
  • “Do you detect queues?” → “No, and we don’t claim to. Your staff already know. We move what they know to everyone who needs it, in three seconds.”
  • “Is this only for events?” → B&B sell event catering explicitly. Ask a B&B person at lunch how big that line is and use their number. Don’t guess it.
  • “What’s next?” → “Today BonaFlow keeps the event flowing. The same stations know what ran out and what didn’t — after the event, that becomes what to cook more of next time.”
